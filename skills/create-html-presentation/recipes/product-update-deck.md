# Product update deck

Read [the shared base](_common.md) first; this recipe adds to it.

## Must

- One self-contained static HTML file, built on the starter's `main.share-presentation` and `section.share-slide` structure. No scripts, navigation buttons or slide controls.
- Every slide fits its 16:9 frame at 1280px wide. If a slide's content doesn't fit, cut words or split the slide; never let it grow taller.
- About 40 words of body text per slide; slide titles about 8 to 10 words.
- Usually 4 to 7 slides. If the user names a slide count, keep to it and say in your reply what didn't fit.
- Use only facts and numbers from the user's material; calculations from them are fine.
- Give a cause only when the material gives it. If the material says the cause isn't known, the slide says so.
- Report only the asks, plans, owners and dates the material contains. Add none of your own.
- Sort every gap in the notes. A choice only the reader can make goes on the slide as a full-sentence question with its reason. A fact the requester knows goes to the requester as a question in your reply, never to the reader. Never leave "TBD", "[date]", "not set" or "to confirm" on a slide.
- Before handing over, render the deck with `check.mjs`, look at every slide, fix what is wrong and check again (see "Check it, fix it, check again").

Create a regular product update that tells a busy leader where things stand against the goals and what is needed from them. It must work as a pre-read, without you presenting, and the reader will compare it with the last one. Slide 1 answers first: in a sentence each, how we're doing against the goal and what we need from the reader; if the material contains no ask, say no decision is needed this time. Every slide title is a claim, so the titles read in order make the update; keep one idea per slide. Lead with outcomes, the results that moved for users or the business; what shipped supports them and never leads. Show every result against its goal or target and against the previous period (or the same period last year) when the material gives them, drawn to scale on one chart with the target marked, and define a measure in plain words where its name could mean several things. If the material says a measure has a known limit, say it next to the number. Reconcile what the last update promised: for each commitment, say whether it was done, slipped (with the new date and the reason), changed, or isn't known; never present silence as done. Spend the slides on the exceptions. Results on track get a line, together on a scorecard; misses, surprises and anything going the other way get the room, each with what changed, why if the material says, and what is being done about it if the material says. Make the headline match the detail: if a total rises while a part of it falls, the same title or slide says both ("Orders rose overall, but repeat orders fell for the second month"). State bad news plainly and early, without spin or apology, and never let a cover slide read greener than the slides behind it. Causes need evidence. When the material lists possible causes, name them as possibilities, say what is being done to find out, and don't build a title, a takeaway or a later slide on one of them. Two things happening at the same time is timing, not a cause. Don't explain what a number means for the business, predict consequences or add a risk, a benefit or a next step the material doesn't state; let the reader draw conclusions from the facts set side by side. When several teams report, build one update: a scorecard of every goal, then the exceptions, not a slide per team. End with the ask as the closing slide's title, with no second large line restating it: what decision or help, from whom, and by when if the material says. When the material asks the reader to choose between options, set out each option with the same facts and give no recommendation unless the requester gave one; if they gave a lean, show it as their lean ("We lean towards…"). When the material contains no ask, close with what happens before the next update. Write like a direct colleague: plain verbs and specific nouns, no marketing tone or hype words, no stacked adjectives, and no lists padded to three items. If the user asks for a tone, such as formal or punchy, keep the facts and the caution and change only the wording and the style preset. Keep it clean but never bare: a scorecard (`.facts` or a table), a chart beside its takeaway (`.split`), two columns (`.cols`), a short sequence (`.steps`) or one short statement set large (`.big`). A slide that is only a title and a few lines of plain text looks unfinished. Use large type, one accent colour, charts drawn as inline SVG with a viewBox, labels on the marks instead of legends, and balanced titles with no single-word last line. Wrap each chart in a `.chart` container, as the starter does. In charts, use `.mark` for the series the slide is about, `.mark-other` for the rest and `.label` or `.label-on` for text. Avoid the AI-default look: rows of identical cards or big-number tiles, icons, gradients, emoji, decorative images and centred text. Nothing meant for you, such as chart coordinates, "source: supplied notes" or "proposed" tags, appears on a slide. The reader has not seen your notes: never mention "the notes" or "supplied" material, and name a source only as the user named it. Keep the starter's look unless the user gives a brand, style or template. Show the draft for review; publishing needs separate approval.

## Layout for this deck

Every slide has a fixed frame: the padding, the footer line with the slide number and, on every slide after the cover, the title at the top in the same place, so a reader flipping through finds each claim where they expect it. Everything under the title goes in one `.stage`, which places it as one block a little above the middle of the space between the title and the footer, so a slide with less on it still looks balanced and stays close to its title. Add `stage-top` to a dense slide, such as a full scorecard, that reads better starting right under the title. Never stretch or space content out to fill the slide. On the cover, the stage holds everything.

This layout is the default; if the user asks for a different one or gives a template, follow theirs.

## Before you hand it over

- Reading only slide 1, does a leader know how we're doing against the goal and what is needed from them (or that nothing is)?
- Do the slide titles, each at most two lines, read in order to make the update, with outcomes before what shipped?
- Is every result shown against its target and the previous period where the material gives them, with each commitment from the last update accounted for?
- Does any title or takeaway read better than its detail, or hide a part going the other way?
- Is every cause, consequence, risk, next step, owner, date and ask on the slides in the material? Is every unknown cause called unknown?
- When the reader must choose, are the options set out alike, with no recommendation the requester didn't make?
- Is each chart an SVG drawn to scale with labels on the marks, and no slide over about 40 words of body text?
- Is there no "TBD", placeholder, mention of "the notes", "proposed" tag, hype or marketing tone?
- Did `check.mjs` report no problems (or only ones you looked at and accepted), and did you look at every screenshot? If you could not render, say so.

## Optional share/artifacts publishing

Publishing is optional and needs the user's separate approval. With a connected share/artifacts client, publish an approved deck with `publish_presentation` and choose who can see it as described in [the publishing notes](../references/share-artifacts.md). Build the deck from this recipe, not from the `presentation-deck` recipe those notes mention. The share/artifacts viewer keeps this recipe's static inline SVG charts (shapes, paths, text, gradients, clip paths and markers).
