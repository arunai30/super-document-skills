Support field note · seat reservations

Seeing a seat
is not holding a seat.

Two customers can see the same last-seat count because browsing uses a recent snapshot. Checkout checks current inventory and holds the seat for only one customer.

Source: Seat-reservation design note, September 8, 2026. This explains the supplied product behavior, not how frequently stale counts occur.

One number. Two very different promises.

Browse card · information

“A seat was available.”

30 secondsMaximum age of the cached count

Opening the card creates no reservation. Another customer may hold the seat before this snapshot changes.

Checkout · inventory change

“A seat is held for you.”

10 minutesLength of a successful checkout hold

The server checks and reserves in one transaction: the check and the hold happen together, so two checkouts cannot both claim that seat.

Worked example · illustrative, not a measured event

Follow the last seat, not just the screen.

Time below is elapsed time in this example. The colored rules distinguish viewing, reserving, and a rejected attempt; the text states each outcome.

  1. 0 s

    Ari sees one seat.

    The browse card shows one available seat. Ari has not entered checkout.

    Available · no hold
  2. 8 s

    Bea sees one seat too.

    Bea sees the cached count. Both screens can be showing the same snapshot without reserving anything.

    Available · no hold
  3. 12 s

    Ari gets the hold.

    Ari starts checkout. The server finds the seat available and reserves it for Ari for up to ten minutes.

    Held for Ari · unavailable to Bea
  4. 15 s

    Bea cannot reserve it.

    Bea starts checkout. The current inventory says the seat is already held, so the server does not create a second hold. This example does not imply Bea was charged.

    Still held for Ari · no hold for Bea

A hold is temporary—not a completed sale.

Successful payment during the valid hold

Held → sold

Ari’s valid hold becomes a sold seat. A later customer cannot reserve it.

Payment fails, or the hold expires

Held → available

The seat returns to inventory. A later checkout can reserve it if it is still available at that moment.

These are the alternative outcomes in the supplied design note. No payment outcome was specified for the illustrative timeline above.

What support can confidently explain

The browse count is an estimate. A checkout hold is a reservation.

When a customer says “but I saw a seat,” explain that another checkout may have reserved it after the displayed snapshot. Confirm whether the customer actually received a hold before describing their seat as reserved.

Avoid promising: that refreshing guarantees a seat, that seeing “1” creates a hold, or that this mismatch means a customer was charged.
